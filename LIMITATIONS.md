# Industrial Engineering Limitations & Plant Deployment Caveats

This document provides a transparent, technically rigorous assessment of the assumptions, boundary conditions, and real-world engineering prerequisites for deploying the **Dead-Cycle Time Optimizer (DCTO)** on a physical industrial extrusion press.

---

## 1. Synthetic Telemetry vs. Physical Field Instrumentation

- **Current Implementation:** Telemetry is generated by a physics-informed discrete-event simulator calibrated to standard 15 MN – 30 MN aluminum extrusion press kinematics. Injected failure modes (valve-overlap delays, micro-stalls, and creeping wear) are deterministic synthetic signals.
- **Plant Reality:** Physical press telemetry is captured by linear variable differential transformers (LVDTs), magnetostrictive position transducers (e.g., Balluff Micropulse), and piezoresistive pressure transducers (e.g., Hydac / WIKA) wired to high-speed PLC input cards (1 ms to 10 ms cycle time).
- **Required Calibration:** Deploying on a production press requires recording 500 to 1,000 nominal production cycles to establish the empirical baseline variance ($\sigma_{nominal}$) per phase under plant conditions before enabling active anomaly alerting.

---

## 2. Hydraulic Oil Temperature & Viscosity Dynamics

- **Current Implementation:** The baseline duration assumes the hydraulic system operates at steady-state operating temperature with constant fluid viscosity.
- **Plant Reality:** Industrial extrusion presses use ISO VG 46 mineral or synthetic hydraulic oil. During a cold start (e.g., Monday morning after weekend shutdown), oil temperature may be ~25°C–30°C (kinematic viscosity $\approx 100\text{ mm}^2/\text{s}$), whereas steady-state operating equilibrium reaches 50°C–55°C ($\approx 32\text{ mm}^2/\text{s}$). Higher viscosity naturally introduces 50 ms to 150 ms of additional valve spool response lag.
- **Mitigation for Real Deployment:** A production detector must ingest the `MainHpuOilTemperature` tag from the PLC and apply a temperature-normalized regression correction:
  $$\tau_{corrected} = \tau_{measured} - f(T_{oil})$$
  preventing false micro-stall alerts during morning warm-up phases.

---

## 3. Extrusion Die & Tooling Variations (Re-Tooling)

- **Current Implementation:** The baseline treats auxiliary dead-cycle phases as identical across consecutive cycles.
- **Plant Reality:** While the auxiliary cycle sequence (`decompression` $\rightarrow$ `container open` $\rightarrow$ `shear` $\rightarrow$ `billet load` $\rightarrow$ `container close`) is largely uniform, changing extrusion dies or container liners (e.g., running 7-inch vs. 9-inch diameter billets, or long 1,000 mm vs. short 650 mm billets) alters the required container stroke distance and rapid advance travel distance.
- **Mitigation for Real Deployment:** The detector should bind to the MES recipe tag (`ActiveDieRecipeID`). When a die change is recorded, the detector should either:
  1. Switch to a pre-calibrated baseline profile linked to that specific tooling recipe, or
  2. Soft-reset the rolling baseline buffer with a 15-cycle adaptation window.

---

## 4. Supervisory Analytics vs. Closed-Loop PLC Control

- **Architecture Boundary:** DCTO is designed as a **Level 2 (Supervisory Diagnostics & OEE Optimization)** system operating via IEC 62541 OPC-UA. It does NOT replace or interfere with Level 1 hard real-time safety interlocks or deterministic motion controllers (Siemens S7-1500 / Beckhoff TwinCAT 3).
- **Closed-Loop Action:** To physically eliminate flagged delays, the insights from DCTO serve two operational purposes:
  1. **Condition-Based Maintenance:** Alerting hydraulic maintenance teams to sticky proportional valve spools (servicing spool seals or replacing proportional pilot valves before catastrophic failure).
  2. **PLC Sequence Optimization:** Providing automation engineers with empirical proof to safely reduce conservative PLC timer interlocks (e.g., trimming a 300 ms debounce timer on a container-open proximity switch down to 100 ms).

---

## 5. Edge Hardware & Network Architecture

- **Current Demonstration:** Runs in Python on a workstation connecting to an asynchronous OPC-UA server on `localhost:4840`.
- **Plant Deployment Architecture:**
  - In a plant environment, Python runtimes are suitable for edge IPCs (e.g., Siemens IPC427E, Beckhoff CX2040) running Ubuntu Core or Windows 10 IoT Enterprise inside the automation cabinet.
  - For millisecond-level cycle event capture, the PLC publishes timestamped transition events directly to the edge IPC via Beckhoff ADS or Siemens S7 protocol over isolated plant Ethernet (PROFINET / EtherCAT), avoiding TCP/IP network jitter.
